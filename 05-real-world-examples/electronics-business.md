# An electronics retailer: truthful product content

> This is a **fictional teaching case**, not an account of actual customer transactions, staff or businesses. Replace example values and claims with verified material before any real use.

![An electronics retailer: truthful product content](../assets/illustrations/product-lighting.svg)

## Fictional brief

An electronics shop wants a product page, a poster and a 30 second demonstration. It has a genuine device, approved manufacturer specifications and its own confirmed price. The exact model is intentionally omitted here to avoid pretending a specification was checked.

### Prompt sequence

```text
Build a product content brief from the supplied official specification and the retailer’s verified price sheet. Separate manufacturer claims, shop specific offers and details not supplied. For the poster, choose no more than two useful verified features. For the video, recommend demonstrations that staff can actually perform with the physical device.
```

### The biggest visual risk

Generated photos may invent camera lenses, ports, screen icons and brand marks. Never use a synthetic render to prove actual specifications. Film the device. Keep the final brand name, price and call to action in editable text. If making an illustrative background, label its role clearly.

### Concrete production order

Confirm the exact model and storage variant. Photograph front, rear and ports. Record a screen demonstration that does not expose private accounts. Script a natural explanation of two real functions. Edit with clear captions and authentic sounds. Ask a staff member to check every word against the manufacturer's documentation.

### Quality review

Compare the visual to the actual device, check charging or compatibility claims and ensure there is no invented warranty. A clean photograph with a good headline is often stronger than a luminous synthetic phone floating in space.

**Result to measure:** Relevant enquiries that cite the stated features, not merely image impressions.

## Questions to discuss after the exercise

1. Does the visual show the correct number of ports, buttons and camera lenses?
2. Is the charger or network compatibility confirmed in the specifications?
3. Which performance claim would need an independent test?
4. Can a customer identify the exact model and its approved warranty terms?

## Extended case: explain a phone without inventing specifications

A fictional electronics retailer has three models available, each with verified storage, charging requirements, price and warranty details. A rushed generator creates a glossy phone poster with an extra camera lens and a promised 'two-day battery' that the specifications never state. That image may be eye-catching but is commercially misleading.

### Prepare a product comparison

Build one table with exact model identifier, verified storage, screen size, supported charging method, current retail price, official image and warranty terms. Mark availability with a timestamp. Treat estimated battery life, camera quality and network performance as conditions requiring evidence rather than promises.

```text
Draft a comparison for three verified models. Use only the approved
specification table and make unknown fields visibly blank. Lead with
the question a buyer is likely to ask: storage, battery charging or
price. Show one practical consideration per model without inventing
performance results. Separate specifications from opinions.
```

### Demonstrate rather than decorate

Film the actual devices in hand under neutral lighting, recording the controls or features customers can see. Show the precise charging connector if that is the topic. Do not replace the product with an image-generated approximation. A simple split screen explaining model identifiers is more useful than a spinning holographic phone whose colour and lens layout change between frames.

**Human voiceover example, subject to verification:** *This model has the storage listed on the box. Before buying, compare the price, warranty and charging connector with the one you use now.* Do not claim a feature that is absent from the source table.

### Owner review

Before publishing, check model naming character by character, actual stock quantity, warranty wording, price and physical product appearance. When a buyer messages about availability, rely on live inventory rather than yesterday's generated caption. Evaluate qualified enquiries and returns related to misunderstood features.
