# Photograph real products and design around them

> **Updated:** 20 November 2026. Interface, account access and available models can change.

![Photograph real products and design around them](../assets/illustrations/product-lighting.svg)

*Original instructional artwork. Diagram labels are conceptual, not screenshots of a vendor interface.*

## A real product is its own source

For a sale, photograph the actual item wherever possible. Use a clean lens, stable camera position, single understandable light source and a background that does not compete. A window and simple board can be more useful than elaborate generated lighting.

### A repeatable three frame set

Frame one shows the complete item at eye level. Frame two reveals one material or feature in close view. Frame three places the item beside a familiar object or a measured ruler so buyers can judge scale. Use consistent white balance.

```text
Plan three product photographs for the actual ceramic mug. The shop has one north facing window, a plain table and a phone tripod. For each frame specify camera height, approximate crop, lighting direction and the customer question it answers. No fabricated decoration or claims.
```

### When generation helps

Image tools can produce moodboards, illustrative backgrounds or early concepts. They are not suitable substitutes for truthful product identity when they change labels, buttons, materials or proportions.

### Editing order

Correct the crop, exposure and colour first. Remove only real distractions. Then add exact brand text, price and contact in an editable design file. Compare the final artwork to the real item before approval.

**Exit test:** A customer would recognise the delivered item from the picture.

## What to retain for the next cycle

Keep original camera files, product reference photographs, retouch notes and final approved exports. Do not overwrite the genuine product record with a generated or composited version.

## Light experiment that teaches more than fifty style adjectives

Set a ceramic mug one metre from a window and take three photographs without changing the mug: at the window edge, with a white board reflecting light onto the dark side, and with the board removed. Examine shadow edge, highlight and the visibility of surface texture. The exercise teaches you to describe what the light actually does. It also gives real assets that can be used in marketing with far less risk than generating a substitute product.

**Three honest commercial crops:** (1) full item with enough room to assess form, (2) close detail of a feature the buyer cares about, (3) actual scale reference. A generated scene can support a moodboard, but the sellable item should stay recognisable. If the tool changes a brand label or material, stop using that image for the product listing.

```text
Study these three genuine photographs of one product. Recommend which
image answers shape, texture and size questions. For each, propose only
crop, exposure and white-balance corrections. Do not add accessories,
change colour, remove a material defect or alter the product geometry.
Give one honest headline based only on the product fact sheet.
```

**Approval:** compare final exported image to the photographed item on a calibrated or reasonably neutral screen. Record the source file and any substantive edits. An art director should be able to explain every visual choice in terms of audience understanding.
